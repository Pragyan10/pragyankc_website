Research

Intent-aware software engineering. In the age of AI.

Software increasingly starts and changes through natural language: feature requests, privacy policies, and short prompts that AI agents turn straight into patches. Along the way, what people actually meant can be lost, misread, or quietly replaced by assumptions. I study where that happens and build LLM methods that catch uncertainty, ask the right questions, and check changes against stakeholder goals.

01

How intent is lost

Empirical studies of Signal, Mastodon, and Moodle, combining repository mining with interviews, show how unclear requests slow development and how requests get lost moving from forums to trackers.

RE 2026 · RE 2025 · CrowdRE 2025

02

LLMs that ask better questions

A human-annotated dataset of feature-request defects and LLM methods that detect ambiguity and incompleteness, then generate clarifying questions that preserve what the requester was after.

RE 2025 · RE Doctoral Symposium 2024

03

Goal-conditioned AI-generated code

At Carnegie Mellon, I extract stakeholder goals from real issues and use them to guide and validate both patches and new tests, evaluated on SWE-bench Verified and SWE-bench-Live.

CMU 2026 · CrowdRE 2026

04

Privacy and secure software

Privacy policies are promises to users. I use LLMs to check those promises against app data flows and to help small teams produce GDPR Article 30 processing records.

SANER 2024 · ESPRE 2024 · PrivateNLP 2020

AI should help developers ask better questions, not silently resolve uncertainty.
